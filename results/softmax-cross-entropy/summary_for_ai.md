# Softmax and Cross-Entropy in Machine Learning: Multi-Class Diagnosis on Real Patient Data, Lung-Cancer Screening and Language Models

**How are softmax and cross-entropy used in machine learning, and when do you need them?** Use a sigmoid for a yes-or-no answer and softmax when the answer is one of several classes: softmax turns the network's score per class into a probability per class, and cross-entropy is the loss that trains it, charging the model the surprise of the true class; its gradient is simply the predicted probabilities minus the one-hot truth. This lesson trains that layer on 366 real dermatology patients with six possible diagnoses (97 percent on held-out patients, at the level of the original 1998 paper), shows the same layer at the end of Google's lung-cancer screening network (Nature Medicine 2019) and of GPT-2's next-token prediction, and demonstrates the four practical failures: over-confidence and calibration, missing inputs at decision time, label noise, and the hidden second-best class.

## Key facts
- **When do I need softmax instead of a sigmoid?** A sigmoid gives one probability for a yes-or-no answer. Softmax gives a probability for each of several classes that add up to one: six diagnoses, a thousand image classes, or fifty thousand possible next tokens.
- **What does cross-entropy measure?** Minus the log of the probability the model gave to the true class, averaged over examples: the model's surprise at the truth. Its gradient with respect to the scores is the predicted probabilities minus the one-hot truth, which is where backpropagation starts.
- **How well does softmax regression do on the dermatology dataset?** Trained on 293 patients and tested on 73 unseen ones, it is right 97 percent of the time and the correct diagnosis is always in its top two, matching the roughly 96 percent reported by the original 1998 study with a different method.
- **What is calibration and why does it matter in medicine?** A calibrated model's 90 percent means it is right about 90 percent of the time. Over-trained networks become over-confident; temperature scaling, one scalar fitted on held-out data, repairs this before deployment, which matters whenever a doctor or a screening programme acts on the probability.
- **How does a lung-cancer screening model use softmax?** A 3-D convolutional network turns a CT scan into scores for no-cancer and cancer, and the softmax gives the probability of cancer that sets the call-back threshold. Google's 2019 Nature Medicine model reached 94.4 percent AUC this way.
- **What are the main ways a softmax classifier fails in practice?** Over-confidence when trained too long without regularisation, inputs that will not exist at decision time (a biopsy the clinic cannot do), noisy labels in the training records, and a single top label hiding a close second-best class.
