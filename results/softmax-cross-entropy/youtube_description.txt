Use a sigmoid for a yes-or-no answer and softmax when the answer is one of several classes: softmax turns the network's score per class into a probability per class, and cross-entropy is the loss that trains it, charging the model the surprise of the true class; its gradient is simply the predicted probabilities minus the one-hot truth. This lesson trains that layer on 366 real dermatology patients with six possible diagnoses (97 percent on held-out patients, at the level of the original 1998 paper), shows the same layer at the end of Google's lung-cancer screening network (Nature Medicine 2019) and of GPT-2's next-token prediction, and demonstrates the four practical failures: over-confidence and calibration, missing inputs at decision time, label noise, and the hidden second-best class.
