Use linear regression when the target is a number (demand, price, duration) and logistic regression when the target is yes or no (default, disease, churn); use softmax for several classes. Both fit a weighted sum of features, logistic through a sigmoid, and both give a readable coefficient per feature, which is why they underwrite credit scoring and clinical risk scores. Fit them first as the baseline, judge them on held-out data with the right metric (RMSE or MAE for a number, AUC and calibration for a probability), and watch for the four classic failures: extrapolation, data leakage, class imbalance with an unchosen threshold, and a non-linear relationship visible in the residuals. This lesson does all of that on 1,000 real German loans and 17,379 real hours of bike rentals.
