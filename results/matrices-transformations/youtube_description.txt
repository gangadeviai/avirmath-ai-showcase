Every layer of a neural network is a matrix times the input vector plus a bias; attention computes all query-key dot products at once as Q times K transpose; an image is a matrix of pixel values and a convolution is a matrix product in disguise. A matrix is best understood as a linear transformation that moves the whole space, with its columns recording where the basis vectors land; matrix multiplication composes transformations, the determinant measures how area scales, and eigenvectors are the directions a matrix only stretches, which is what limits the learning rate. Google's PageRank is an eigenvector, Netflix-style recommenders factorise the user-by-film matrix, and LoRA fine-tunes large language models with low-rank matrices.
