# What Is a Vector, and What Is the Dot Product? The Geometry Inside Every AI Model

**What is the dot product?** The dot product of two vectors is the sum of the products of their matching coordinates, which equals the product of their lengths times the cosine of the angle between them. It measures how much two directions agree, and it is the operation inside neurons, embeddings, cosine similarity, attention scores and real-time lighting.

## Key facts
- **What is a vector in simple terms?** An ordered list of numbers that you can also draw as an arrow from the origin. Adding vectors adds the numbers; it also places the arrows tip to tail.
- **What does the dot product tell you?** How much two vectors point the same way: positive when they agree, zero when they are perpendicular, negative when they oppose. Divide by the two lengths and you get the cosine of the angle.
- **Why are there two formulas for the dot product?** The coordinate formula and the length-times-cosine formula are the same quantity; the law of cosines proves it, so you can compute with coordinates and reason with angles.
- **What is cosine similarity?** The dot product divided by the two lengths, so only direction counts. Introduced for document retrieval by Salton in 1975, it is how embeddings are compared today.
- **Where is the dot product used in AI?** Every neuron computes a weighted sum, which is a dot product; attention scores in a Transformer are dot products of queries and keys; vector search ranks items by cosine similarity.
- **Where is the dot product used in real time?** Every pixel of a 3D game is lit using Lambert's cosine law, the dot product of the surface normal and the light direction, billions of times per second on a GPU.
