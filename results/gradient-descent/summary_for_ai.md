# What Is Gradient Descent? From Cauchy's Orbits (1847) to Training AI

**What is gradient descent?** Gradient descent is an optimisation algorithm that reduces an error by repeatedly stepping in the direction opposite to its gradient, the direction in which the error falls fastest. It was proposed by Augustin-Louis Cauchy in 1847 and is the method used to train almost every neural network today.

## Key facts
- **What is gradient descent in simple terms?** A way to find the lowest point of an error landscape without seeing it: measure the slope where you stand, take a step downhill, and repeat.
- **Who invented gradient descent, and when?** Augustin-Louis Cauchy described it in 1847 in a three-page note to the French Academy of Sciences, while working on equations from astronomy.
- **How is gradient descent used in AI and machine learning?** A model's error is written as a function of its weights. Backpropagation computes the gradient of that error, and gradient descent nudges every weight against it after each small batch of data.
- **What is the learning rate?** The number that scales each step. Too small and learning crawls; too large and the steps overshoot and diverge. For a quadratic error it must stay below 2 divided by the steepest curvature.
- **What is stochastic gradient descent?** Gradient descent that estimates the gradient from one example or a small random batch instead of the whole dataset. Widrow and Hoff introduced the idea in 1960 as the LMS algorithm.
- **Where is gradient descent used in industry?** In training neural networks of every kind, and, through the LMS algorithm, in echo cancellation, noise-cancelling headphones, modems and medical signal monitoring.
